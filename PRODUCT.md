# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: hiring managers and recruiters screening for junior UI/UX roles. They arrive from a LinkedIn link or resume, skim fast, and decide in under a minute whether to read a full case study or move on. Their job-to-be-done is risk reduction: confirm this candidate can think through a design problem, ship real craft, and communicate process clearly.

Secondary (deep-dive, not primary): senior designers and design peers who open a case study end-to-end and judge rationale, trade-offs, and depth.

The design must serve both altitudes: a skimmable first pass (hero, work list, outcomes) and a rewarding deep read (case-study narrative). Skim wins ties.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Joaquin Pacia, a UofT graduate (High Distinction) positioning as a UI/UX designer with a multimedia-production and front-end-development background. The site IS the product: it argues, by its own craft, that Joaquin can design. Success = a recruiter reaches "contact" convinced, or opens a second case study.

The portfolio carries three or more MDX case studies (UX research, brand video, app design), a hero, an about section, a CV/experience timeline, a tools marquee, and contact links.

## Brand Personality

Clean and precise. Restraint over decoration. Whitespace, systematic spacing, confident typographic hierarchy. The designer stays mostly invisible so the work speaks, but the about voice carries a light human warmth ("fun is equal to success") that keeps it from reading sterile.

Three words: precise, calm, credible.

Voice: plain, specific, no buzzwords. Lets outcomes and process do the persuasion.

## Anti-references

- **Generic template portfolios.** Wix/Squarespace-shaped, interchangeable, no point of view.
- **Dribbble-bait / over-designed.** Flashy gradients, fake dashboards, style over substance.
- **Corporate SaaS landing.** Hero-metric template, identical icon-heading-text card grids, navy + gradient slop.
- **Dark-mode dev-bro neon.** Terminal-green-on-black, neon accents, "hacker" cliche. (The site has a dark default, but it must read as quiet and editorial, never neon.)

Craft direction (aim toward): Linear / Vercel tech-minimal. Tight type, restrained color, generous space, precise exponential-ease motion.

## Design Principles

1. **Skim first, depth on demand.** Every page rewards a 30-second scan before it rewards a full read. Hierarchy guides the eye to the proof.
2. **The site is the portfolio piece.** Craft of the site itself is evidence. Sloppy spacing or a janky animation is a design failure, not a cosmetic one.
3. **Restraint as confidence.** Remove before adding. Whitespace and one deliberate accent beat decoration. If an element does not earn its place, cut it.
4. **Show the thinking, not just the pixels.** Case studies lead with the problem and the decisions, not a gallery of screens. Process is the differentiator for a junior hire.
5. **Quiet in the dark.** Dark default must feel editorial and calm, never neon or "hacker." Tinted neutrals, controlled contrast.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: contrast on text and UI in both themes, visible focus states, keyboard-operable nav and lightbox, alt text on case-study imagery. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` on the tools marquee and other animations. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning.
